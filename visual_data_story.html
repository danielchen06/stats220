IC3 Study Space: A Visual Data Story

Observational data collected in IC3, University of Auckland — March & May 2026

Introduction

IC3 — the Information Commons on Level 3 — is one of the University of Auckland’s most popular study spaces. With over 200 seats spread across a quiet, open-plan floor, it is the go-to destination for students wanting to focus on coursework, complete assignments, or simply find a desk during busy periods.

This visual data story explores how IC3 is used throughout the day: How crowded does it get? Does the time of day affect seat availability? What are students actually doing when they’re there — and how noisy is it really?

Data was collected through personal observation across multiple dates in March and May 2026, logging free seat counts, activity types, noise levels, and the time each observation was made. A total of 43 observations were recorded.

Key question: When is IC3 most crowded, what are students doing there, and how noisy does it actually get?

When Is IC3 Most Crowded?

The first question any student asks before heading to IC3 is: will I find a seat?

The bar chart below shows the average number of free seats observed during morning, afternoon, and evening sessions. Mornings tend to have the most availability, while afternoons — when the bulk of university timetabled classes end — see IC3 fill up significantly.

This pattern makes intuitive sense: students often arrive after morning lectures to work through the afternoon. If you want a guaranteed seat, mornings before 12pm are your best bet. Afternoons between 12pm and 6pm are consistently the most competitive for seating.

How Do Free Seats Vary Throughout the Day?

Looking beyond broad time-of-day categories, we can examine exactly which hours tend to see the most or least availability, by plotting observations against the actual hour they were recorded.

The trend line reveals a clear dip in availability between roughly 10am and 5pm — the core of the university day. Late evening observations (after 7pm) consistently show more free seats, as student numbers thin out. Two notable outliers with very high free seat counts (around 90–100) were recorded during weekend mornings and a weekday evening in March, confirming that off-peak times genuinely offer much more space.

What Are People Doing — and How Loud Does It Get?

Not all study behaviours create the same atmosphere. The heatmap below maps the combination of observed activity and noise level, showing how often each pair was recorded across all 43 observations.

The pattern is reassuring for anyone seeking a quiet study environment: the overwhelming majority of IC3 observations pair laptop studying with silent or quiet conditions. Socialising — while present — is relatively rare and tends to accompany moderate, not loud, noise. IC3 lives up to its reputation as a serious, low-noise study space. The single observation of “Loud” noise coincided with a socialising group, which aligns with expectations.

Conclusion

The data tells a consistent story: IC3 is a reliable, quiet study space that is busiest during the afternoon and overwhelmingly used for focused laptop work. For students planning a study session, the takeaway is clear — arrive in the morning or evening to maximise your chances of finding a seat in a peaceful environment.

Data source: Personal observational data collected in IC3, University of Auckland, March–May 2026. All 43 observations were recorded individually using a Google Form.
